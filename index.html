Working Papers:
  • Evaluating the Spillover Effects of Public Infrastructure on Local Labor Markets: Evidence from India
    Solo authored · Working Paper, 2026
    [Draft PDF] · [Replication Code]
    Abstract
    This paper investigates the direct and spatial spillover impacts of targeted rural connectivity programs on agricultural-to-non-farm occupational transitions and real wages across contiguous districts. Utilizing staggered rollouts and high-resolution administrative data combined with PLFS micro-data, I find substantial spatial reallocation of informal labor with heterogeneous impacts across skilled versus unassisted households.
  • Credit Access, Informality, and Firm Growth in Developing Economies
    With co-authors · Under Review
    [Draft PDF] · [Data & Code]
    Abstract
    We study how regulatory thresholds and targeted priority-sector credit affect transition margins between informal and formal micro-enterprises. Using regression discontinuity designs on administrative enterprise surveys, we show that credit availability mitigates liquidity crunches but interacts with local regulatory enforcement to shape long-run formalization choices.

Some things I believe:
  • Causal identification without institutional knowledge is fragile
    • Econometric estimators are only as good as the understanding of the institutional data-generating process
    • Spending time reading government notifications and fieldwork documentation pays higher returns than obsessing over fragile higher-order polynomials
  • Good economics raises the ceiling, not just documents the floor
    • Pointing out welfare losses is easy; identifying feasible institutional mechanisms that unlock productivity is where economists add real value
    • Development economics is fundamentally about removing structural frictions so people can realize their agency
  • Replication and open science must be default
    • Every empirical claim should have clean, documented code and publicly accessible synthetic or raw data wherever legally permissible
    • Unreproducible results pollute the collective knowledge base and misguide policy
  • Speed of iteration beats perfection in early stages
    • Plot the raw data first before running complex regressions
    • Contact with empirical facts forces you to kill bad hypotheses early; write down your core mechanism in simple equations before building large models
  • Economic incentives explain much, but culture and norms constrain the feasible set
    • Institutions that work in textbook models often stall because informal social contracts dominate legalistic enforcement
